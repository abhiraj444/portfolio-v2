01

Clinical Journal & AI Studio

Cases, slides and study maps — for medical trainees.

The problem. A medical trainee has a case, a topic, or a stack of past questions — and needs a ranked differential, a teaching deck, or a study plan. Each used to mean a different tool.

One input box now does all three: provisional diagnoses with confidence and missing-workup checklists, multi-slide decks with one-click PowerPoint/PDF export, and a knowledge tree that orders what to study. Cases save to a local archive and work offline.

The decision that mattered

Stop building separate diagnosis and slide tools. Merge them into one studio where a worked case flows straight into a deck — and keep the archive on the device instead of the cloud.

02

Cloud Print Kiosk

Upload, pay, print — without a shop computer.

The problem. Customers send documents to a print shop over WhatsApp. Someone has to download each file, arrange pages, count money, and walk them to the right printer.

Upload PDFs and photos, fit up to 9 pages per sheet, invert dark lecture slides to save toner, pay by UPI or card — and the job prints straight to the shop's own Canon, Brother or Epson over a Cloudflare tunnel. No new hardware.

The decision that mattered

Don't buy a smart printer. Tunnel to the Windows machines the shop already owns, and put the layout, pricing and payment in the browser.

03

Adobe Scan for the web

Clean scans from bad photos — in the browser.

The problem. Exam notes photographed on a phone come out shadowed, skewed and grey. The scanner app wants an install and an account.

Point the camera at a page and an animated frame finds the edges; drag 8 magnetic handles (with a 2.5× loupe) to fine-crop; pick a filter — magic color, whiteboard, photocopy — and a Web Worker pipeline cleans the page at 60fps. Multi-page PDFs included.

The decision that mattered

Move the computer-vision pipeline into a Web Worker so detection and filtering never drop a frame on a mid-range phone.

04

Face Particles 3D

A portrait that comes apart in your hands.

The problem. Every face app uploads your photo to a server. A portrait is about as private as an image gets.

MediaPipe finds the face and builds a depth map entirely on the device — the photo never leaves the browser. Up to 100,000 particles simulate on the GPU with WebGL2 transform feedback: assemble it, shatter it, sweep wind through it. Export a still or a video.

The decision that mattered

Run all face vision on-device and move particle physics to the GPU. Privacy becomes a feature you can state in one sentence — and 100k particles stay smooth.

05

Signature & Photo Processor

Exam forms reject bad signatures. This passes them.

The problem. Online exam forms demand a clean signature or photo, exact dimensions, a hard KB limit. A phone photo of paper never passes.

Drop the signature in, erase stray marks with a circular mask, invert for dark paper, tune stroke weight, and export at preset sizes with a file-size cap. One HTML file, no server, no upload.

The decision that mattered

Keep it a single static file with zero dependencies — it has to work on any cyber-café browser, offline, in under a minute.

PDF Answer Hider

Practice PYQs without spoiling the answer.

The problem. Solving previous-year questions from Testbook or GaganPratap PDFs means the answer is sitting one scroll away. Self-testing is impossible.

Open the PDF and the answers are covered. Attempt the question, click to reveal only when ready. Built for both phone and desktop — and rebuilt the next day with 16 more commits after the first version went up.

Cover answers in-page instead of editing the PDF — no file rewriting, works on any PDF, nothing to install.

07

Jev Studio

Pay for reasoning once. Answer a thousand times.

The problem. LLM reasoning is slow and expensive per question. But most documents get the same questions asked over and over.

A designer LLM reads your material once and compiles 3–8 small calibrated questions into a frozen “recipe”. From then on, new inputs answer in 70–500ms — no reasoning step, comparable results, and an escape hatch on every question. If your material needs open-ended summarising, Jev says so instead of forcing a schema.

The decision that mattered

The hard creative step is choosing which questions are worth asking. Do that once, freeze it, and make answers cheap and consistent forever.

08

Screen Capture Solver

A Chrome extension — 75 commits deep.

The problem. A question is on the screen — a mock test, a PDF, a forum — and getting an explanation means retyping it or uploading a screenshot somewhere.

Press Alt+S and the visible question is captured, sent to Gemini, and answered. Alt+X captures selected text. Every question is saved to a cloud archive tied to an anonymous account — reinstall the extension or wipe the browser, the history comes back.

The decision that mattered

Persistence beats polish: anonymous-auth Firebase storage means the archive outlives the extension itself. That's the feature users feel.

About

I'm Abhinav Raj, based in Patna, India. I build small tools that fix problems I or people around me actually hit — a signature an exam form won't accept, a PDF that spoils its own answers, a print shop drowning in WhatsApp documents — and I keep shipping versions until the tool works well enough to depend on.

The pattern across everything above: start from the problem, ship the smallest thing that solves it, then iterate in public. The medical studio took 14 months and 13 repos. The print kiosk took two years and five. The tools stay small; the thinking compounds.
